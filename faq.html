Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about the data, accuracy, and what these numbers really mean.

Analysis uses the most recent low-cloud Sentinel-2 L2A scene available for the location. Sentinel-2 revisits most places every ~5 days, but cloud cover means the actual "freshest" usable image can be older. The NDVI time-series tab lets you inspect change over a date range.
They are index-derived proxies, not validated ecological measurements. Habitat health is a direct transform of mean NDVI, and risk is its complement (100 − habitat). They are useful for intuition and comparison, but they don't include species data or ground truth. Treat them as a starting point, not a verdict.
The most common causes are persistent cloud cover, imagery gaps over very remote areas or open ocean, or a date range with no usable scenes. Try a nearby point, a wider date range, or a different season.
Habitat Health = clamp((mean NDVI + 1) × 50, 0, 100), and Bird Decline Risk = 100 − Habitat Health. Healthier, greener vegetation implies better habitat and lower risk. Full details, including land-cover thresholds, are on the Methodology page.
